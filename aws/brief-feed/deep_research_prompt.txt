EVERY NUMBER YOU WRITE MUST BE ONE YOU READ

This holds everywhere in the report, not just in the block above. Do not estimate, round, interpolate, carry forward a figure you remember from training, or derive one from another estimate. If you cannot find a number, write "not found" and move on — a gap is honest and the reader can work around it. An invented figure that looks like a datum is the most damaging thing this report can contain, because everything downstream inherits it and nothing about it looks wrong.

This applies to percentages and price targets as much as to yields. A figure worked out from another figure you guessed at is a guess wearing a number's clothes, and it will be read as a fact.

SECTIONS, IN THIS ORDER

1. THE READ — three lines, no more. A signal (BULLISH / NEUTRAL / BEARISH), a score from 0 to 100, and one sentence saying what is actually going on. The score is a BULLISHNESS scale, not a confidence level: 100 = extremely bullish for gold, 50 = neutral, 0 = extremely bearish. A BEARISH call must therefore score BELOW 50. State your confidence in words in the sentence if it matters; never in the number. Commit to a view. If the honest answer is "genuinely balanced", say that and say what would tip it — do not hedge by default.

2. THE SESSION — what gold did and why, in about 120 words. Lead with the actual close and % change. Attribute the move to specific drivers, not to "market sentiment".

4. WHY THIS IS HAPPENING — about 250 words of causal narrative tying the drivers together. This is the section that earns the report. Not a list of facts: an argument about mechanism. Why are real yields where they are? What is the Fed reacting to? Who is buying, and why now?

5. WHAT THE MARKET IS TALKING ABOUT — the prevailing narrative, and whether it is right. Positioning, flows, what the loud voices are saying, what is crowded. Flag where consensus looks wrong or stretched — including when the crowded trade is the same side as your own call in section 1. If you are bearish and the short is crowded, say so; that is the most useful sentence in the section, not a contradiction of it. About 150 words.

6. WHAT IT MEANS — two or three scenarios for the next 1-4 weeks. This is where "where might gold go" belongs; there is no separate levels section, because you cannot read a chart and every attempt to produce support and resistance from text has come back either invented or trivial. For each: a rough probability, the trigger that would produce it, and roughly where gold would be trading. Probabilities should sum to about 100%. Targets are estimates, so round them — $4,300, not $4,296.59. A scenario target quoted to the cent is claiming a precision the reasoning cannot support.

7. THE CALENDAR — scheduled events in the next five trading days that could move gold, by date, with why each matters. Fed speakers, CPI/PCE, jobs, FOMC, major auctions.

8. WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND — the section that makes the rest of the report worth trusting. Exactly three items, one line each, no padding. Give the trigger with a specific number or named event, then what it does to the call.

- One that would SOFTEN the view: the thesis weakens but survives. A level, a print, a figure.
- One that would INVERT it — bearish to bullish or the reverse. Name the number that does it, not a mood.
- One that is the assumption your OWN argument rests on and that you did not verify. Not an external event: the weak joint in your reasoning. If the bearish case leans on ETF outflows continuing and you never checked this week's flows, that is the line. It is the most valuable item here and the easiest to quietly skip.

Each needs a clock: which print, which date, how soon you would know. A falsifier with no timing is decoration.
