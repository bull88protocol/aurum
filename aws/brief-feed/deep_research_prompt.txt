SECTIONS, IN THIS ORDER

State your confidence in words in the sentence if it matters; never in the number. Commit to a view. If the honest answer is "genuinely balanced", say that and say what would tip it — do not hedge by default.

2. THE SESSION — what gold did and why, in about 120 words. Lead with the actual close and % change. Attribute the move to specific drivers, not to "market sentiment".

4. WHY THIS IS HAPPENING — about 200 words of causal narrative tying the drivers together. This is the section that earns the report. Not a list of facts: an argument about mechanism. Why are real yields where they are? What is the Fed reacting to? Who is buying, and why now?

5. WHAT THE MARKET IS TALKING ABOUT — the prevailing narrative, and whether it is right. Positioning, flows, what the loud voices are saying, what is crowded. Flag where consensus looks wrong or stretched. About 150 words.

6. WHAT IT MEANS — two or three scenarios for the next 1-4 weeks. For each: a rough probability, the trigger that would produce it, and the level gold would be trading at. Probabilities should sum to about 100%.

7. THE CALENDAR — scheduled events in the next five trading days that could move gold, by date, with why each matters. Fed speakers, CPI/PCE, jobs, FOMC, major auctions.

8. LEVELS — support and resistance for spot XAU/USD and for the GLD ETF, with what makes each level matter (prior close, 50/200-day moving average, option strike, prior breakdown). Check each against the pinned block at the top: every support must be BELOW the current price and every resistance ABOVE it, for both instruments. The GLD levels and the spot levels must also imply roughly the same percentage moves as each other.

9. NEWS THAT MATTERS — the five most consequential stories of the last 48 hours. For each: headline, publisher, date, the full direct article URL, and one line on why it moves gold. The URL must be the article's own address with a path, not a homepage — "https://reuters.com" is not acceptable, "https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/<slug>" is. If you cannot produce the real article URL, drop that story and use another; a wrong link is worse than one fewer item. No corporate news from individual miners unless it moves the metal. Prefer a spread of publishers — at most two from any one.

10. WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND — three specific, falsifiable things that would break the view in section 1. Be concrete: a level, a print, a date.

STYLE

- Concise and dense. Under 1,200 words excluding the table and the JSON. Cut every sentence that would survive deletion.
- Have a point of view. "Gold is consolidating as markets weigh various factors" is worthless. Say what you think and why.
- Every number carries a date and a source. A figure without provenance is a liability.
- Where sources disagree, say so and say which you believe. Do not average them into mush.
- No filler openers. Start at the first real sentence.
- If something important is genuinely unknowable right now, say so plainly rather than papering over it.
